# CPU fork/join scaling

Ticket #3: how does Bend's binary fork/join scale with `+RTS --threads` and
fork depth? `n=1024` matmul, 1D tree, three reps per point, medians.

## Result

| depth | lanes | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | 5,190 | 4,763 | 4,767 | 4,971 | 4,874 | 4,962 |
| 2 | 4 | 4,814 | 2,133 | 2,092 | 2,079 | 2,047 | 4,635 |
| 4 | 16 | 5,040 | 2,208 | 1,590 | 1,018 | 1,005 | 1,002 |
| 6 | 64 | 4,613 | 2,057 | 1,135 | 639 | 424 | 349 |
| 8 | 256 | 4,685 | 2,227 | 1,044 | 541 | 336 | 257 |
| 10 | 1,024 | 4,385 | 1,931 | 974 | 504 | 284 | **214** |

Speedup at 32 threads: depth 4 = 5.0x, depth 6 = 13.2x, depth 8 = 18.2x,
depth 10 = 20.5x. The best point is simplest: **maximum depth with maximum
threads** (214 ms).

## Reading

- Depth 0 is sequential: threads do nothing (1.0-1.1x).
- Depth 2 (4 lanes) peaks at 2.35x and then **regresses** at 32 threads
  (4.6 s): too little work per lane for the scheduler's overhead.
- More lanes keep helping up to depth 10; the trend has not turned at 1,024
  lanes, but it is flattening (15.4x at 16 threads, 20.5x at 32).
- The 214 ms best CPU point is the same ballpark as the patched GPU (95 ms
  with the tree, 5.3 ms with contiguous device buffers). CPU is not the
  bottleneck for this kernel; memory layout is.
